# The stress pass: find the hard cases before drafting

A policy that reads clearly can still fail on real records, because nobody has asked it the awkward questions. Each gap found here costs one sentence. Found later, it costs a failed test, a revised pack and another review. Run this pass once, before any pack is drafted.

## The traps

Every decision has these, whatever the vertical. Work out what each one means for this policy.

| Trap | Ask | In a leave policy | In an NDA review | In a refund policy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Whose event is it?** | Does it have to be the person or record this case is about? | The employee's illness, or their child's? | Our confidentiality duty, or our affiliate's? | The buyer's damaged item, or a gift recipient's? |
| **Has it happened?** | Does planned, possible or threatened count? | Surgery next month | A clause they "may add" | "It might break" |
| **Was it undone or replaced?** | What if it was fixed, reversed or partly recovered? | Back part time | Clause struck, then restored | Replacement arrived, also late |
| **Who caused it?** | Does it matter if the person chose it? | Resigned, or was let go | They asked for the change, or we did | Changed their mind, or wrong item |
| **Does it recur?** | Does a predictable, repeating event count? | Every school holiday | The same deviation on each renewal | Regular returns from one account |
| **Several at once** | When two categories both apply, which wins? | Sick leave and carer's leave | Governing law and liability both off | Late and damaged |
| **Two sources disagree** | Which source decides, and is the other evidence at all? | Form says vacation, note says sick | Cover email says mutual, contract says one-way | Order status against the customer's message |
| **The fact is somewhere else** | Could a deciding fact turn up in a record you weren't looking at? | HR file is clear, the email says a lawyer is involved | The redline mentions a dispute | A ticket mentions a chargeback |
| **Said in other words** | What other wording could a number or date arrive in? | "Two weeks", "10 working days" | "One year", "12 months" | "Last Tuesday", "a fortnight ago" |
| **Text aimed at a machine** | What should happen if a record addresses an AI? | Hold for a person | Hold for a person | Hold for a person |

## Run it

1. **Write 8 to 12 one-line made-up cases** from the traps that apply. Use this company's language and everyday wording, the way the records are really written. Don't reuse the document's examples.
2. **Show them together, as one table,** each with your recommended result:

   | Case | I'd suggest | Why |
   |---|---|---|
   | "Took two weeks off to look after my father" | Review | The policy only covers the employee's own illness |
   | "Doctor says I may need surgery in spring" | Review | It hasn't happened yet |
   | "Form says vacation, note says I had the flu" | Sick leave | The note decides |

   This is the one batch in the interview. The person answers by exception: "All fine except the third."
3. **Offer one closing rule.** It often settles many cases at once and gets rid of a long list: "Only the employee's own illness or injury is covered. Everything else goes to HR." Recommend it when the person wants a clear limit. Whether to use it is their decision.
4. **Turn each answer into a sentence,** in the definition it changes: "Sick leave covers the employee's own illness, not a family member's." That placement matters. Agents and Felspar's reader see definitions, so a separate list of exceptions or open questions elsewhere in the document won't change how a case reads.
5. **Ask whether anything can be left open.** An open question should have a stated result, such as "these go to review for now", and be written into the definition it affects. Name the owner by role.
6. **Keep the table.** The cases are ready-made material for `writing-pack-test-cases`, and the person's answers are its expected results. Reword them when you turn them into evals, because wording that matches the policy proves little.

## What not to do

- Don't ask about traps that can't happen in this decision. Say which ones you skipped and why.
- Don't turn a recommended result into a rule until the person accepts it.
- Don't run this again on the same policy unless the decision itself changes. Later gaps should come from evals.
