# Policy template

The shape of a document Felspar can draft a pack from. Use it to spot what's missing and as a blank starting point when nothing is written. It is a checklist for the owner's document, not a form to fill in mechanically: keep the company's own headings, order and words where they already work.

## Sections

1. **Purpose.** One or two sentences: why the policy exists and what it decides.
2. **Applies to / not for.** Which cases it covers, and which it doesn't (and where those go instead).
3. **What a case is.** One sentence saying what a single case is, and how it's identified (an invoice number, a ticket number, a candidate's email plus the opening).
4. **Definitions.** One short entry per category the decision depends on. Each has a definition, what it includes, what it doesn't include (a near miss), and an example. Include a **"none of these"** category for cases that clearly fit none of the others, and say where they go.
5. **Rules, in order.** One rule per sentence, in the order they're applied. The first rule that fits decides.
6. **Thresholds.** Exact numbers with their unit: amounts with a currency, counts with what they count, time in days. Say which date the count starts from and whether the edge itself counts ("on or before day 30").
7. **Exceptions.** Each one as its own rule, with when it applies.
8. **When it's unclear.** What happens when information is missing or the rules don't settle a case, and the kind of review that takes it. Name the kind of review (a finance review), not a person or a channel.
9. **Required wording.** Only if replies or outputs must say, or must never say, something specific. Write it word for word.
10. **Examples.** Two or three real (or realistic) cases and the outcome each got, including one near an edge.

## Worked example

A short policy for a fictional company's expense approvals. It shows the shape; the numbers are invented for the example and are not a recommendation.

> ### Expense approval policy
>
> **Purpose.** This policy decides whether an employee expense claim is approved for repayment.
>
> **Applies to.** Expense claims submitted in the expense tool by employees. It does not cover supplier invoices, which follow the invoice approval policy.
>
> **What a case is.** One expense claim, identified by its claim number (for example EXP-20418).
>
> **Categories.**
>
> - **Travel:** transport and lodging for a work trip. Includes train, flight, taxi and hotel costs. Doesn't include commuting between home and the usual office. For example: a train ticket to a client workshop.
> - **Meals:** food and drink during a work trip or with a client. Includes a team lunch with a client present. Doesn't include a team lunch with no client present. For example: dinner on the second night of a conference.
> - **Equipment:** items needed to do the job. Includes a replacement laptop charger. Doesn't include software subscriptions, which go through IT. For example: a headset for calls.
> - **Other:** claims that clearly fit none of the categories above. They go to a finance review.
>
> **Rules.**
>
> 1. Claims submitted more than 60 days after the expense date are declined, unless the exception below applies.
> 2. Claims over 50 EUR without a receipt get a request for the receipt.
> 3. Meals over 60 EUR per person go to a manager review.
> 4. Equipment over 300 EUR goes to a manager review.
> 5. Claims in the Other category go to a finance review.
> 6. All other claims are approved.
>
> **Thresholds.** Days are calendar days counted from the expense date; a claim submitted on day 60 is on time. Amounts include tax.
>
> **Exceptions.** Claims from a trip where the employee was unwell for more than 3 days may be submitted up to 90 days after the expense date.
>
> **When it's unclear.** If the category or amount can't be told from the claim, it goes to a finance review.
>
> **Required wording.** A declined claim's reply must say: "This claim was submitted after the 60-day limit." It must never promise repayment before approval.
>
> **Examples.**
>
> - A 42 EUR taxi to a client, with a receipt, submitted after 10 days: approved.
> - A 75 EUR dinner for one, with a receipt: manager review.
> - A claim submitted on day 61 with no exception: declined.

## What makes it draftable

- Every category has a definition, an inclusion, an exclusion and an example.
- There is a "none of these" category with a destination.
- Every number has a unit and an exact edge.
- Every rule is one sentence, and the rules are in order.
- Unclear cases have a named kind of review.
- Required wording is written word for word.
